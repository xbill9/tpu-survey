11: ## Native numeric format support — the table that decides quantization strategy
12: 
13: | Generation | bf16 | int8 | **fp8** | Notes |
14: | :--- | :---: | :---: | :---: | :--- |
15: | v5e | yes | yes (2x bf16) | **no** | Google publishes bf16 and Int8 peaks only |
16: | v5p | yes | yes | **treat as no** | The v5p page lists bf16 **and FP8 at the same 459 TFLOPs**. Equal peaks buy no compute win even if the path is real, so no quantization conclusion changes. Unmeasured here — see the v5p note below |
17: | v6e (Trillium) | yes | yes (2x bf16) | **no** | same pattern — verified 2026-08-10, see below |
18: | v7 (Ironwood / TPU7x) | yes | yes | **yes** | first TPU with fp8 in the MXU |
19: 
20: **This is the single most consequential fact for quantization work.** On v5e and v6e:
21: 
22: - **int8 is the only low-precision format with a compute win.** Both generations quote int8 at exactly
23:   2x their bf16 peak, the signature of a genuinely native MXU path.
24: - **The v6e fp8 absence is now confirmed at the source, not inferred.** Google's v6e page lists exactly
25:   three peak-compute rows — `bf16: 918 TFLOPs`, `Int8: 1836 TOPs`, and a per-Pod bf16 figure — and **no
26:   fp8 row anywhere** (checked 2026-08-10). This was previously read off a second-hand spec set.
27: - **fp8 is storage-only.** Values widen back to bf16 before the matmul, so fp8 can buy footprint and
28:   bandwidth but never FLOPS. A benchmark showing no speedup from fp8 on these chips is the expected
29:   result, not a misconfiguration.
30: - **4-bit has no MXU path either.** int4 weights buy footprint and bandwidth, then unpack for compute.
